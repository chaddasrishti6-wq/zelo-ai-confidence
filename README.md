# zelo-ai-confidence
# Built at Code; Without Barriers (Microsoft, IWD) 

**Note:** Intentionally playful visual interface to lower intimidation barriers for the target audience

**Title:** Zelo

**Tagline:** Build confidence your way, in your time

**Project Description:** Zelo, an AI confidence builder, aims to diversify by demystifying first. It's a hybrid application based on observed features from apps like Instagram, Snapchat, TikTok, Facebook, Kahoot, Quora, Reddit, Duolingo, etc that make an app compelling, engaging, and inclusive. Versatile features have been integrated, including a feed with posts, articles to read, daily challenges (which encourage users to try without AI first to promote responsible, moderated use), debates, and hot takes sections to encourage discourse. Further features, like tutes, acknowledge the engagement of a reels-based format over YouTube to enable uploading 30-60-second videos on different AI skills, as well as more light-hearted content, like gardening or memes. The stories and challenges sections curate a communal and inclusive experience, where one can share updates (like Instagram stories) and engage in games to learn more about AI interactively (like Kahoot or Quizlet). There is also an Explore and Shop section - the first compiles courses and certifications users can attempt, while the other is based on an online-shopping vision for AI tools. Coins can be gained through interacting with the app, enabling the purchase of different tools. I understand that this would require financial support from potential sponsors, but this prototype is built with a vision that I believe is tangible and worth investing in. The profile and notifications emulate the social media format to foster the communal principle by which this project was developed - demystify to diversify, ensuring that the app is accessible.

**Prototype Share Link:** Live prototype was built using GitHub Spark. Not currently publicly hosted, but happy to walk through it live. 

**Value Proposition:** Zelo was envisioned with a utilitarian mindset; the greatest good for the greatest number of people, catering to different age groups and demographics in mind. For teenagers and university students like myself, Zelo aims to address the confusion of not knowing where to start, laziness in wanting to explore, or feeling helpless or lost. IT combines the interactivity of Instagram to curate a platform that is enjoyable and makes learning about AI feel less like a structured, constricting course. For the elderly or those who live more domesticated lives (like househusbands or housewives), Zelo enables users to keep up-to-date and mitigate "feeling too dumb", "being too behind", and "not knowing enough." Features like a toggle activate a larger font-size, making it more accessible and ergonomic for certain age and demographic brackets, which was something I wanted to include whilst thinking about my grandparents. There is also a light mode/dark mode feature to appeal to different aesthetics. The ability to scroll through certifications and shop satisfies the online shopping experience, whilst keeping course opportunities in one place to alleviate the feeling of being lost amid vast information and exponential technological growth. Ultimately, in mind of people, Zelo's principle is simple; if we seek to diversify, we must first demystify and encourage AI literacy, leverage responsible use, and ensure accessibility and inclusivity in the process.
